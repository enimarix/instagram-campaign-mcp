# Viral hooks

Built from 13 videos by 7 creators (evidence in `sources/viral-hooks/`). Last updated 2026-11-25.
Support counts are **distinct creators**, not videos: one creator repeating an idea counts once.
All sources are expert opinion with hand-picked view counts; none shows an A/B test. Treat the
rules as strong defaults, then check them against our own analytics.

## How to use this

**Write hooks for a topic**
1. Check the topic first: would the target viewer care if the hook were plain? If not, change the
   topic, not the wording. Find the key visual for the first 3 s; no visual and no way to make
   one means rethink the idea.
2. List the facts/angles. Pick the one with the biggest contrast (what they believe now vs what
   is true / possible).
3. Write 10 hooks across several archetypes and formulas below (2-3 short lines each).
4. Run every hook through the checklist. Drop anything that fails a box.
5. Keep the best 3. For each, write the on-screen text hook, describe the first frame, and write
   the 2-4 lock-in lines that follow.

**Grade a draft someone gives you**: go through the checklist line by line, name each weak spot
(soft opener, vague, no context, no contrast, not about the viewer, can be misread), give a grade
A-F, then 3 rewrites that fix those exact weak spots.

## Rules (strongest first)

- **Say what the video is about in the first 1-2 seconds.** Viewers swipe on autopilot and
  retention falls hardest in the first two seconds; nobody stays for a video they can't place. No
  throat-clearing, start clips at the action, give the first bit of value in or right after the
  hook. (6: K, DB, HD, IG, LS, ML)
- **One reading only, fewest simple words.** Short active sentences, about 6th-grade words, no
  wordplay. Test the hook alone: if it can be understood two ways, rewrite. Shorter sentences in
  the hook, longer later. Read it aloud ~5 times. Compression, not fast talking. (6: K, AA, DB, IG,
  LS, ML)
- **Plant a question the viewer needs answered.** Context plus an open loop: after the hook, ask
  "what question pops into the viewer's head?" No question means rewrite. Keep the answer hidden
  (in words, text and the first frame) until late, and open a new loop when one closes. (6: K, AA,
  DB, HD, IG, LS)
- **The payoff must deliver what the hook promised, or more.** Bait the video doesn't pay off
  makes viewers leave and not come back. Over-delivering beats clickbait. (6: K, DB, HD, IG, LS, ML)
- **Contrast is the engine of curiosity.** Show the gap between what the viewer believes (A) and
  your alternative (B) that is faster, better, cheaper or surprising. Challenging a common belief
  is one of the strongest hooks. Contrast can be stated (name A and B) or implied (name only B).
  Turn words ("but", "however", "despite", "although") signal the twist. (5: K, DB, HD, IG, LS)
- **Be specific.** Exact numbers, amounts, times and results beat vague ones ("$97,000 in 13
  days", "22 days", "7,292 mi"). No hedges ("nearly"): make the claim absolute or find a stronger
  angle. Bigger concrete outcome wins. (5: K, AA, DB, IG, ML)
- **Spoken words, on-screen text, visual and audio must say the same thing.** From the visual
  alone you should roughly guess the words, and the other way round. Mismatch causes confusion and
  swipes; this is the most common mistake. (5: K, HD, IG, LS, ML)
- **The on-screen text hook is its own piece of writing.** It must work muted. Few words (3-5 big
  words, max 2 lines), broken at natural phrase breaks, no punctuation except quotes/brackets, no
  "and/but/that". It names the subject plus one of: a pain, a desired outcome, or something that
  just changed. It tells what the viewer can't see yet (stakes, what's about to happen), not what
  is already on screen. Keep it ~3 s, about a third down from the top, inside the safe zone, on a
  clean part of the frame. (5: K, HD, IG, LS, ML)
- **The first frame is part of the hook.** A clean frame with one focal subject, visual contrast
  that supports the question, and "just enough" motion (a lean in, moving text). Too much
  motion overwhelms, none bores; big effects look gimmicky. (4: K, HD, IG, LS)
- **The lines right after the hook decide if they stay** ("lock-in zone", ~5-10 s). Confirm the
  hook's claim immediately, back it with proof (result, screenshot, study, credential), and keep
  a turn ("but how...?") coming. (4: K, DB, LS, ML)
- **A weak topic can't be saved by wording.** Pick topics with built-in stakes (risk, money,
  health, a person in need, a famous name). Abstract concepts and jargon you can't show flop.
  (4: K, DB, HD, IG)
- **Make it about the viewer.** "You/your" instead of "I/me". Name a pain they already have (a
  need-to-have, not a nice-to-have) or their exact situation ("If you've been lying in bed
  scrolling for the past hour"). Know what your audience believes, or you can't challenge it.
  (3: K, HD, ML)
- **Use emotional, niche-specific words.** Keep a bank of the words your niche reacts to
  (fitness: shredded, lean, fat loss...). Use casual words people say to a friend. 1-3 word power
  phrases amplify the pain or outcome. One or two words can change the result. (3: K, AA, ML)
- **Model proven hooks, never copy them.** Collect hooks from videos far above their channel's
  average (50K+ likes, or outliers), in your format. Strip each to its structure, refill with your
  topic and words. Phrases that recur across several winners are safest. (3: K, AA, ML)
- **Borrow familiarity.** A famous person, brand or cultural reference makes a niche topic feel
  known. But never open on an unknown person's name: say what they did first. (3: K, DB, IG)
- **Lead with the benefit, then the subject.** "If you want better sleep, take magnesium" beats
  "Magnesium is one of 21 core minerals". (1: K)

## Formulas

Archetypes (any topic can be written in all six; pick the one with the most contrast for your best
visual) (1: K):
- **Fortune teller** (now vs future): "[X] is going to change [field] forever."
- **Experimenter** (peer demo): "I just tried [X] for [time], and [surprising result]."
- **Teacher** (lesson): "If you're [doing X], this is the best way to [outcome]."
- **Magician** (stun first, then any other type): "Check this out." / a striking visual or sound.
- **Investigator** (secret): "This is a secret [thing] ... but it's not just any [thing]."
- **Contrarian** (against common belief): "Everybody loves [X], but [it's overhyped]." / "You're doing [X] wrong."

Structures:
- **Context lean, interjection, snapback**: line 1 states the topic and pulls them in (benefit,
  pain, metaphor, mind-blowing fact); line 2 "But [stun]"; line 3 the twist in the opposite
  direction, same topic. (1: K)
- **Building blocks**: [subject] + [action] + [result, as surprising as possible] + [contrast
  A to B] (+ [proof, e.g. "again"]) (+ [time, e.g. "in 90 days"]). Proof and time can move to
  line 2. (1: K)
- **Claim + amplifier**: [claim or stat]. [comparison or reframe that makes it bigger]. (1: K)
- "Most people [solve X with A], but [B] does it [N times faster]." (stated contrast) (1: K)
- "If you want [benefit], [don't do Y,] do [Z]." (1: K)
- "Here's how to [big result] in [small input]" ("30 days of content in 30 minutes"). (1: K)
- "Here's how to stop [vivid pain]." / "If you're [pain, specific], here's [outcome]." (1: ML)
- "[Status they want] and I still [thing they think is forbidden]" ("Lean at 45 and I eat carbs
  every day"). (1: HD)
- "Despite [setback], [person] [surprising action]" / "[Person] thought [X]. However, ..." (1: IG)
- "Do you know why [famous person] [did surprising thing with big number]?" (1: DB)
- "[Person] thinks [X], so I'm going to prove them wrong by [challenge with a constraint]." (1: LS)

Good/bad pairs and more examples: [references/examples.md](references/examples.md).

## Checklist

- [ ] Topic is clear within 1-2 s, from the words and from the text alone (sound off).
- [ ] Only one way to read it; simple words; nothing to cut.
- [ ] A viewer who knows nothing about us still gets it (no missing context, no unknown names).
- [ ] It's about the viewer ("you"), a pain or outcome they care about.
- [ ] There is a contrast or a question they now need answered, and the answer isn't given away.
- [ ] At least one specific number, result or concrete detail; no hedges.
- [ ] Words, text, first frame and audio say the same thing.
- [ ] The video really pays it off, and the next 2-4 lines confirm it with proof.
- [ ] Not a generic teaser, a series label, or a gimmick standing in for substance.

## Don't

- Vague teasers with no context: "This is the craziest thing I've ever seen", "Who can relate?".
  (2: K, HD)
- Open on series branding ("Day 12 of...", "Episode 4"): it delays the value. (2: K, ML)
- Lean on gimmicks (red arrows, zooms, jump effects) instead of a clear, interesting idea.
  (3: K, IG, ML)
- On-screen text that describes what the viewer already sees. (1: IG)
- Show or name the answer in the first frame (it closes the loop). (1: HD)
- Open on a flat fact, even a wild one; frame a character or stakes and save the record for later.
  (1: IG)
- Copy a hook word for word from a distant niche; the psychology and grammar don't transfer.
  (1: K)
- Never use: fake comments from a second account, taboo or stereotype bait, "scam" accusations
  about real people for attention. Seen in the sources, rejected: deceptive or harmful.

## Conflicts

- **Questions or statements?** Kallaway's data from 4 channels says confident statements beat
  questions. Bitton's and Igor's examples are question hooks with millions of views ("Do you know
  why...?", "Have you ever...?"). Likely: a question works when it is specific and the viewer
  can't answer it; a generic question loses. (K vs DB, IG)
- **Which layer matters most?** Kallaway says the key visual (two videos), then in another says
  the text hook matters most and visuals least. Igor says text + visual beat voiceover in the first
  second. All agree they must match. (K vs K, IG)
- **Is the hook 80% of the result?** Kallaway and Mino Lee say yes. Igor says topic comes first:
  "a hook can only do so much" with a weak clip. (K, ML vs IG)
- **Does a winning hook transfer across niches?** Mino Lee: format matters more than niche and
  transfers. Kallaway: copying from a distant niche fails; match the topic type. (ML vs K)
- **Where does the payoff go?** Bitton: deliver it, then end at once. His guest: around the third
  quarter. (DB vs DB's guest)
